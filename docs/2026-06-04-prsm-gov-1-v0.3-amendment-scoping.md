## 1. Purpose

This scoping document proposes amending PRSM-GOV-1 from v0.2 to v0.3 to address three gaps that have been identified through downstream architectural work in the Prismatica ecosystem.

The three gaps:

1. **Quadratic voting mechanism is not specified.** The v0.2 charter references "token-weighted staker vote" (line 112) and "on-chain governance" (line 277) for FTNS-holder votes but does not define the weighting formula, vote thresholds, quorum, voting period, or implementation. Without this specification, token-holder ratification cannot be enacted as a binding governance step.

2. **Scope of token-holder ratification is undefined beyond Phase 7+.** Line 277 names "Phase 7+ staking parameters, certain economic-policy votes" as token-holder-ratified, but "certain economic-policy votes" is not enumerated. Whether parameters affecting Prismatica's protocol-side behavior (e.g., retention split for FTNS earned by major operators) require token-holder ratification is currently ambiguous.

3. **Sybil resistance is deferred without timeline.** SUPPLY-1 line 51 explicitly defers Sybil resistance to a future PRSM-SUPPLY-2 standard. If quadratic voting becomes operational without Sybil resistance, the voting mechanism is trivially gameable (wallet-splitting amplifies effective vote weight).

All three gaps are intertwined: quadratic voting without Sybil resistance is structurally weak; Sybil resistance without a clear vote scope is academically interesting but not actionable; vote scope without weighting mechanism is incomplete.

---

## 2. Why v0.3 now

Three reasons to address these gaps in a coordinated v0.3 amendment rather than incrementally:

1. **Prismatica's anti-enshittification design depends on it.** Downstream architectural work (`Prismatica_Foundation_Trust_Layer_Spec.md` §4 retention_config governance) currently specifies Foundation board supermajority + 30-day notice. If quadratic FTNS-weighted token-holder ratification is the canonical model, that spec needs amendment. Anti-enshittification analysis at scale ($500M revenue) materially differs between the two models. The earlier the canonical model is locked, the more durable the downstream design.

2. **Foundation formation is approaching.** v0.2 is explicitly pre-Foundation-formation. Once Foundation is formed and the v1.0 charter ratification process begins, voting mechanics need to be operational. Drafting v0.3 now allows the design to be tested in the v0.2 → v1.0 ratification process itself.

3. **The bootstrap phase is the right time.** Per v0.2 §8, the founder holds bootstrap-phase Foundation board authority Years 0-3. Anti-extraction commitments (including voting mechanism specifications) are easier to lock in during the bootstrap phase before Prismatica's economic footprint creates entrenchment pressure.

## 4. Quadratic voting mechanism — recommended specification

This section proposes the design that v0.3 would formalize. Each design choice is presented with options and a recommended default with rationale.

### 4.1 Vote weight formula

**Decision:** How are votes weighted by FTNS holdings?

**Options:**
- (a) Linear: vote weight = FTNS balance
- (b) Quadratic: vote weight = sqrt(FTNS balance)
- (c) Capped quadratic: vote weight = sqrt(min(FTNS balance, cap))

**Recommended default: (b) Quadratic.** vote_weight = floor(sqrt(FTNS_balance_at_snapshot)).

**Rationale:** Linear voting favors whales; capped quadratic introduces an arbitrary cap that's hard to justify. Pure quadratic with proper Sybil resistance is the canonical mechanism in protocol governance literature. With Sybil resistance, the sqrt function provides appropriate dampening of large holders without arbitrary cliff effects.

### 4.2 Vote threshold

**Decision:** What percentage of quadratic-weighted votes cast is required for passage?

**Options:**
- (a) Simple majority (>50%) of votes cast
- (b) Supermajority (2/3) of votes cast
- (c) Tiered: simple majority for routine; supermajority for substantive

**Recommended default: (c) Tiered.** Mirror v0.2 §9.2 thresholds: simple majority for amendments to ratified standards; supermajority (2/3) for v1.0 ratifications or amendments affecting implementer certifications; supermajority (2/3) for retention_config changes.

**Rationale:** Consistency with existing v0.2 board-vote thresholds; differentiates routine operational changes from structural changes.

### 4.3 Quorum / minimum participation

**Decision:** What minimum participation is required for vote validity?

**Options:**
- (a) No quorum (vote valid regardless of participation)
- (b) Fixed quorum (e.g., minimum 5% of all FTNS holders cast votes)
- (c) Adaptive quorum (quorum based on rolling 12-month average participation)

**Recommended default: (b) Fixed quorum at 10% of quadratic-weighted maximum.** Quorum = sum of quadratic-weighted votes cast must equal ≥10% of the sum of sqrt(FTNS_balance) across all FTNS holders.

**Rationale:** Fixed quorum is operationally simpler than adaptive. 10% threshold is achievable for substantive matters but high enough to prevent stealth governance attacks (small minority pushing through changes during low-attention periods).

### 4.4 Voting period

**Decision:** How long is the voting window open?

**Recommended default: 14 days minimum, 30 days for v1.0 ratifications.** Begins after proposal advances past committee review (per §9.2 step 4).

**Rationale:** Matches v0.2 multi-sig 14-day notice. Sufficient time for token holders to evaluate without unduly delaying governance.

### 4.5 Snapshot mechanism

**Decision:** When is the FTNS balance for vote weighting determined?

**Options:**
- (a) Vote-time balance (susceptible to flash-loan attacks)
- (b) Snapshot at proposal time (resists flash loans)
- (c) Time-weighted average over preceding 30 days (anti-volatility)

**Recommended default: (b) Snapshot at proposal time.** Block at proposal submission becomes the canonical balance state for all vote weight calculations.

**Rationale:** Industry standard. Flash-loan attack resistance is non-negotiable for any meaningful governance system. TWA is more complex without adding meaningful security.

### 4.6 Vote scope (decision taxonomy)

**Decision:** Which decisions require token-holder ratification vs board-only?

**Recommended taxonomy:**

| Decision class | Authority |
|---|---|
| Standards ratification (v1.0) | Board supermajority + token-holder supermajority |
| Standards amendments (routine) | Board simple majority + token-holder simple majority |
| Standards amendments (cert-affecting) | Board supermajority + token-holder supermajority |
| Charter amendments (core sections) | Board supermajority + token-holder supermajority |
| Charter amendments (non-core) | Board supermajority only |
| `retention_config` changes (Prismatica retention split parameters) | Board supermajority + token-holder supermajority |
| FTNS emission parameter changes | Board supermajority + token-holder supermajority |
| Phase 7+ staking parameters | Token-holder simple majority (per v0.2 §277) |
| Treasury transactions >$250K | Board only (operational) |
| Research grants | Board only (operational) |
| Certification grants/revocation | Board only (with public review period) |
| Trademark and domain decisions | Board only |
| Foundation board director elections (community seats) | Token-holder vote (per v0.2 §112) |

**Rationale:** Operational decisions stay with board for speed and competence. Substantive economic and standards-level decisions require token-holder ratification to prevent board capture. `retention_config` is explicitly added to token-holder ratification scope to address Prismatica anti-enshittification at scale.

## 5. Sybil resistance — recommended specification

Quadratic voting without Sybil resistance is structurally broken. v0.3 must specify Sybil resistance mechanisms or specify a deadline for PRSM-SUPPLY-2 to ship those mechanisms before quadratic voting activates.

### 5.1 Sybil resistance mechanisms (multi-layered)

**Recommended default: multi-layer defense.**

Layer 1 — **Minimum FTNS threshold per voter:** Voters must hold minimum 100 FTNS at snapshot to cast a vote. Below threshold, vote not counted (but FTNS balance still counted in quorum denominator for token-holder participation rate purposes).

Layer 2 — **Time-lock on new wallet creation:** Vote eligibility requires wallet to have held FTNS for minimum 90 days prior to snapshot. Prevents creation of fresh wallets just before vote.

Layer 3 — **Stake-bonded identity (per future PRSM-SUPPLY-2):** When PRSM-SUPPLY-2 ships, integrate identity binding so each verified identity may hold at most one voting wallet. PRSM-SUPPLY-2 candidate mechanisms include proof-of-personhood (Worldcoin, Self.xyz, BrightID class) and stake-bonded identity attestation.

Layer 4 — **Statistical Sybil detection:** Foundation runs ongoing statistical analysis of voting patterns to flag suspected Sybil clusters; flagged wallets subject to delayed voting eligibility or community review.

### 5.2 Phased activation

**Recommended phasing:**

- **v0.3 ratification (target Q3 2026):** Layers 1 (min threshold) + 2 (time-lock) ship with v0.3.
- **Phase 7+ activation (target 2027-2028):** Quadratic voting activates with Layers 1+2 active.
- **PRSM-SUPPLY-2 ratification (target 2028-2029):** Layers 3 (stake-bonded identity) and 4 (statistical detection) ship as additional defenses.

**Rationale:** Layers 1+2 are sufficient defense for the initial governance load expected in Phase 7. As economic stakes scale, Layers 3+4 add stronger defenses. This phasing matches the realistic timeline for Sybil resistance research.

## 6. Proposal flow

This section proposes the end-to-end flow for a token-holder-ratified governance change under v0.3.

```
1. Proposal submission
   - Proposer: Foundation board member, working group chair, or token holder
     with minimum 10,000 FTNS stake
   - Submission deadline: any time
   - Public on-chain submission to Governor contract

2. Pre-vote period (≥30 days)
   - Public review + discussion
   - Working group / committee review
   - Counsel review (where applicable)
   - Proposer may amend during pre-vote period; amendments restart clock

3. Board advancement vote
   - Board votes to advance proposal to token-holder ratification
   - Threshold: simple majority of full board
   - Failure to advance = proposal dies; can be re-submitted after 90-day cooldown

4. Token-holder snapshot
   - Block at advancement vote becomes canonical balance snapshot
   - Snapshot data published on-chain

5. Token-holder voting period (14 days)
   - On-chain voting via Governor contract
   - Quadratic vote weighting per §4.1
   - Quorum + threshold per §4.2-4.3
   - Sybil filters per §5.1

6. Result announcement
   - Vote outcome published on-chain + Foundation transparency endpoint
   - Failed votes: 90-day cooldown before re-submission

7. Effective date delay
   - 30 days minimum for routine; 60 days for substantive changes
   - Affected parties (Prismatica, other implementers) notified

8. Enactment
   - Foundation multi-sig executes any required on-chain actions
   - Effective date noted in StandardsRegistry
```

**Total elapsed time:** ~75-105 days for routine proposals; ~120-150 days for substantive proposals. This is appropriately slow for substantive governance; faster mechanisms (emergency amendments per v0.2 §9.4) remain available for time-critical matters.

## 7. Implementation architecture

### 7.1 Smart contract design

**Recommended:** Custom Governor contract derived from OpenZeppelin Governor + GovernorVotesQuadraticFraction extension. Deployed on Base alongside existing Foundation contracts.

**Contract components:**
- **GovernorPRSM.sol** — main governance contract; manages proposals, voting periods, thresholds, enactment
- **FTNSQuadraticVotes.sol** — vote weight calculation with quadratic formula + snapshot
- **SybilFilter.sol** — implements Sybil resistance Layers 1+2 (minimum threshold + time-lock)
- **GovernorTimelockController.sol** — enforces effective-date delays before on-chain enactment

**Existing FTNSGovernance.sol in `/archive/templates/`** can serve as reference architecture but requires substantial revision; the archived version lacks quadratic voting + Sybil resistance.

### 7.2 Off-chain vs on-chain

Voting is on-chain by default. Off-chain signaling (Snapshot-style polls) may be used for advisory votes but is non-binding.

**Rationale:** On-chain provides cryptographic auditability matching the Foundation Trust Layer transparency posture. Off-chain signaling is acceptable for pre-vote sentiment gathering.

### 7.3 Integration with existing contracts

The Governor contract integrates with:
- **StandardsRegistry.sol** — Governor writes ratified standards on enactment
- **Foundation Safe multi-sig** — Governor outputs can be executed by multi-sig for treasury or contract upgrades
- **EmissionController.sol** — Governor manages tokenomics parameter changes
- **`retention_config` contract** (Prismatica-side, future) — Governor can update Prismatica retention split parameters when scope warrants token-holder ratification

## 12. Open items

1. **Quorum threshold (10% proposed in §4.3).** Open to working group review. Higher quorum = more protection from minority capture but harder to achieve participation. Lower quorum = more agile governance but more capture risk.
2. **Vote weight formula (pure sqrt vs capped quadratic).** Pure sqrt is canonical but creates infinite-marginal-vote at sqrt(0) → sqrt(1); minimum threshold (Sybil Layer 1) compensates.
3. **Minimum FTNS threshold per voter (100 FTNS proposed).** Affects accessibility for small holders.
4. **Wallet time-lock duration (90 days proposed).** Trade-off between accessibility for new users and Sybil prevention.
5. **PRSM-SUPPLY-2 dependency.** If PRSM-SUPPLY-2 slips beyond 2029, do Layers 3+4 still ship? Or does v0.3 quadratic voting operate indefinitely with only Layers 1+2?
6. **Snapshot block timing.** At proposal submission or at board advancement? Affects FTNS holder behavior during pre-vote period.
7. **Emergency amendment process.** v0.2 §9.4 allows emergency amendments by board unanimity. Does v0.3 add token-holder emergency veto? Or does emergency amendment authority remain board-only?
8. **Vote receipt + audit trail.** What's published per vote? (Vote choice, weight, voter address? Anonymous aggregate?) Privacy vs transparency trade-off.
9. **Override mechanism for vendor-down scenarios.** If Governor contract has a bug, how does Foundation patch? Multi-sig admin path?
10. **Treasury implications.** Does Foundation hold FTNS that can be used for voting? Per v0.2 §4.3 #2, Foundation must not favor any single implementer — Foundation FTNS voting power could be restricted to abstention by charter rule.
